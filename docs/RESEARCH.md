# Realism research notes (2026-11-05)

What engines (Unreal/Niagara, Unity HDRP/VFX Graph, CoD AW post) do that we do not, ranked by visual payoff per line of code for this library.

1. **Soft particles / depth fade** (NVIDIA SoftParticles 2007, UE "DepthFade", URP SoftParticles): particles do not write depth, so the fragment reads the opaque scene depth and fades alpha over a transition zone (smooth curve, not linear) where the sprite nears a surface. Kills the hard line where smoke, rings, glows and beams cut into the ground or the body. Also a near-camera fade.
2. **Screen-space refraction for shockwaves and heat** (grab-pass on a mesh, not a full-screen warp): a ring/heat mesh writes a distortion offset; the final pass samples the scene at uv+offset. Rings read as pressure waves instead of glowing lines; fire gets heat haze. Mask with radial falloff + noise so the edge is irregular; fade by distance.
3. **Energy-conserving bloom** (Jimenez SIGGRAPH 2014, HDRP): no hard threshold (threshold breaks energy conservation), wide low-intensity pyramid (13-tap downsample, tent upsample), bright pixels only bloom because they are bright in HDR. Cheap alternative with three's UnrealBloomPass: threshold ≈ 0.6–0.8, radius up, strength down, and keep all emitters' HDR values honest.
4. **Lit smoke** (six-way lightmaps in Unity VFX Graph; UE uses lit translucency): sprites need a normal. Cheapest stand-in without baked maps: sphere normal from sprite uv (n = (uv*2-1, sqrt(1-r²))) → N·L from the effect light + hemisphere ambient + back-light rim (light behind → bright edge). Gives smoke volume and self-shadow feel.
5. **Blackbody fire** (Blender Principled Volume, Planck's law): map temperature to colour (1000 K red → 2000 K orange → 3000 K yellow-white), intensity separately in HDR; dark soot at the top. Fire core should exceed 1.0 so bloom/tone-mapping do the glow, not a white sprite.
6. **Inverse-square light falloff** (UE particle lights): physically based falloff with a radius window; intensity in HDR units; a flame light flickers at 8–15 Hz with low amplitude; combine with volumetric term (cheap: a soft additive sprite "glow" around the light with inverse-square alpha).
7. **Rings / energy shapes** (realtimevfx.com): fresnel is for curved shapes; flat rings need erosion noise that grows over life, UV-panned noise, a second darker alpha layer for contrast, scroll speed fast→slow over life (dissipating energy), and thin width tapering.

Sources: NVIDIA SoftParticles whitepaper; Unity URP/HDRP bloom docs (energy conservation, Kawase/dual filter); Unity VFX Graph six-way lighting; Epic UParticleModuleLight (inverse-squared falloff); Jimenez "Next Generation Post Processing in Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare" (SIGGRAPH 2014); realtimevfx.com threads on rings/erosion/distortion; Blender Principled Volume (blackbody).
